# ND-014.4 — Peak, breaking, DC and steady-state current policies

**Status:** Partial implementation; software regression is green, normative coverage remains gated.  
**Target edition:** IEC 60909-0:2026, Edition 3.0.  
**Baseline:** ND-014.3, 444 tests.

## Current implemented subset

- The initial symmetrical current remains supported for the four bolted bus-fault types using the existing sequence-network engine and maximum/minimum study setup.
- Peak current is isolated as a pure strategy and remains restricted to maximum, single-fed, radial, three-phase network-feeder cases (Clause 8.1 subset). Multiple-fed (8.2), unbalanced (8.3) and meshed peak methods are not inferred.
- Three-phase network-feeder breaking current is distinguished between single-fed (9.1.5) and multiple-fed (9.2) cases.
- For unbalanced faults supplied only by network-feeder voltage sources, `Ib` is exposed as the maximum phase RMS current from the sequence-network solution (Clause 9.3 network-feeder subset). Machine and converter contributions remain unsupported.
- Three-phase network-feeder steady-state current distinguishes single-fed (11.2.5) and multiple-fed (11.2.6) cases.
- For unbalanced faults supplied only by network-feeder voltage sources, `Ik` is exposed as the maximum phase RMS current from the sequence-network solution (Clause 11.3 network-feeder subset). Machine and converter contributions remain unsupported.
- `first_pole_contact_separation_time_s` is explicit, validated study input.
- A supplemental R/X exponential DC-envelope estimate may be returned for the maximum, single-fed, radial, three-phase network-feeder class. Its trace identifier is `PFA-ENGINEERING/...`, not an IEC clause method. The numerical estimate is not reported as a verified IEC 60909-0:2026 Clause 10 quantity.
- The existing thermal `Ith` / Joule-integral calculation remains limited to the baseline maximum, single-fed, radial, three-phase feeder subset.

## Deliberate limitations

- Clause 8.2 multiple-fed peak-current methods and Clause 8.3 unbalanced peak-current methods are not calculated. The single-fed radial factor is not extrapolated.
- Source-specific Clause 9 methods for synchronous/asynchronous machines, doubly-fed asynchronous generators and full-size converters are not implemented.
- Clause 9.3 is limited to network-feeder voltage sources; no machine or converter decrement is modeled.
- Full Clause 10 DC and unbalanced asymmetrical breaking procedures are not asserted. The supplemental R/X estimate is provided separately and the normative quantity status remains unavailable.
- Clause 11 source-specific rotating/current-source procedures are not implemented; Clause 11.3 remains limited to network feeders.
- Generalized Clause 14 `m`/`n` thermal methods are not implemented beyond the existing baseline subset.
- Three-winding transformer IEC correction and line-interior IEC fault calculation are not integrated.

## Traceability and interpretation

`current_method_references` identifies the strategy used for calculated IEC quantities and separately identifies the engineering waveform supplement. `unavailable_quantity_reasons` records quantities that are not claimed as normatively calculated. A numeric supplemental estimate may coexist with an unavailable IEC status for the corresponding normative quantity; this is intentional and prevents a non-verified estimate from being mistaken for IEC compliance.

These method identifiers are implementation trace labels, not a declaration that all normative details have been independently validated. Independent analytical benchmarks and comparison against the licensed final edition remain required.
